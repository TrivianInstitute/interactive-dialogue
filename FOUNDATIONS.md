# Foundations

## Why dialogue matters

A conversation can reveal an unclear goal, open a possibility, surface a disagreement, or help someone explain an idea. These are educational opportunities. They become learning when the person engages with the material and can carry something into another situation. Frequent turns, polished prose, and a sense of being understood are insufficient evidence by themselves.

This curriculum treats engagement with machine intelligence as a relationship with responsibilities: explain intentions, listen carefully, examine claims, respect boundaries, and preserve the freedom to disagree or leave. This educational orientation does not require a claim about AI consciousness or human equivalence.

## Working definitions

| Term | Meaning here | What to observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interactive dialogue | A responsive exchange shaped by interpretation, contribution, and revision | Later turns respond to specific earlier content |
| Metacognition | Monitoring understanding and adjusting how one learns | A learner notices a gap and changes strategy |
| Agency | Practical ability to define, revise, refuse, and enact goals | The learner makes and explains a consequential choice |
| Calibration | Alignment between confidence and demonstrated accuracy | Confidence is compared with independently checked answers |
| Verification | Checking a claim against suitable evidence | A source is opened and assessed for actual support |
| Repair | Correcting misunderstanding, scope drift, or an unwanted interaction pattern | A boundary or interpretation is restated and checked |
| Transfer | Applying learning in a different situation | A new task is completed without copying the exchange |

These are curriculum definitions. Agency in particular has broader philosophical and scientific meanings; the classroom indicators do not exhaust it.

## A proposed practice cycle

1. **Orient:** State the goal, what you already think, and what remains yours to decide.
2. **Contribute:** Offer an attempt, question, observation, or alternative.
3. **Interpret:** Explain what you heard; check a consequential ambiguity.
4. **Test:** Compare alternatives, inspect evidence, or attempt an example.
5. **Choose:** Accept, revise, reject, defer, or stop for a stated reason.
6. **Carry forward:** Close the exchange and demonstrate what remains independently.

This cycle is a Trivian educational design proposal, not a validated protocol. Repeat or skip steps when appropriate. A simple factual lookup may need very little dialogue; high uncertainty may call for outside expertise. More conversation is not automatically better.

## Conditions that make participation possible

Allow written, spoken, diagrammatic, and assisted participation. Agency does not require doing everything unaided: people may retain access accommodations while demonstrating their own reasoning. Agree on what assistance is allowed for each learning check.

An optional pause can help a learner notice fatigue or overload. Looking away, changing position, taking a comfortable ordinary breath, or choosing no body-focused activity are all acceptable. Bodily sensations cannot establish whether an AI answer is true. Avoid treating ease, eloquence, or speed as evidence of understanding.

## When to end or change the exchange

Stop when the task is complete, the time limit is reached, sources cannot be checked, the exchange keeps repeating, or the learner wants to leave. An unresolved question is a valid outcome. Seek appropriate human expertise for decisions outside the learning setting. A conversational instruction can guide a response; it cannot enforce privacy, persistent memory, permissions, or external system behavior.

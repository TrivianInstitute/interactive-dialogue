# 05 — Agency, boundaries, and repair

**45 minutes · Outcome:** retain and explain authority over a consequential choice.

## Learn

Agency appears in setting the goal, selecting among alternatives, rejecting a suggestion, changing course, and deciding when to stop. Accepting good advice can express agency when the learner understands and endorses the reason. Disagreement alone is not proof of independence.

Before a conversation, distinguish advice, drafting, and external action. Write what may happen and who must authorize it. If the system has external capabilities, permissions must be governed in that system; words in a chat are not an access control.

## Activity: the plan that took over

Scenario: you request ideas for a two-hour volunteer activity. The fictional AI proposes a six-week program and says it can invite the volunteers immediately.

1. **5 min:** Write your original goal, constraints, and reserved decisions.
2. **10 min:** Mark scope drift and the proposed external action. List who else would be affected.
3. **10 min:** Write a repair: “Return to a two-hour activity. Offer two options within that limit. Do not send invitations; I will review the draft.”
4. **10 min:** Compare two fictional options and choose, revise, defer, or decline. Explain the tradeoff using your own criteria.
5. **10 min:** Write an exit statement and share the artifact.

## Exit check

Submit a goal, authorization boundary, repair turn, and justified final choice. Name one condition that would make you stop or consult someone else.

**Facilitator:** Keep the scenario fictional; send no actual invitations. Assess the learner's reasons, including reasonable acceptance. If a conversation becomes uncomfortable, allow withdrawal without requiring an explanation. Never require personal relationship disclosures to demonstrate agency.

# 01 — What dialogue makes possible

**45 minutes · Outcome:** transform an exchange into a responsive inquiry.

## Learn

A request for an answer can be useful. Dialogue adds opportunities to examine the question and contribute to its development. The critical move is responding to specific content: “Your example assumes everyone has internet access; how would the plan change without it?” This brings the learner's observation into the exchange.

The ICAP framework distinguishes kinds of cognitive engagement [R1](../research/REFERENCES.md). Adapting this framework to AI conversation is a curriculum inference; counting chat turns does not show that the learning conditions studied in ICAP are present.

## Activity: three versions of one task

Task: design a one-hour community event where people can learn something together. Materials: paper and the [worked examples](../examples/worked-dialogues.md); live AI is optional.

1. **5 min:** Write your own first plan and one uncertainty.
2. **8 min:** Read or request a finished plan. Mark one useful idea and one assumption.
3. **10 min:** Conduct three turns. Supply your own alternative, ask about a constraint, and respond to a specific suggestion. Offline, a partner proposes a plan; label this as human practice rather than an AI test.
4. **7 min:** Revise your plan and annotate whose contribution led to each change.
5. **5 min:** Compare the versions. Which change has a defensible reason? Which remains untested?
6. **10 min:** Share and complete the exit check.

## Exit check

Write one responsive turn, identify the content it responds to, and state how it could change the plan. Explain when a direct answer would have been enough.

**Facilitator:** Assess contribution and responsiveness, not verbosity. If participants merely request longer answers, invite them to supply an example or counterexample of their own. A participant may prefer the first plan if they can explain why.

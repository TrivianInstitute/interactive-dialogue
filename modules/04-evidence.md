# 04 — Disagreement and evidence

**50 minutes · Outcome:** change or retain a conclusion based on checked support.

## Learn

A fluent response may fit your view while remaining unsupported. Research on sycophancy documents agreement-seeking behavior in studied AI systems [R7](../research/REFERENCES.md). Inviting disagreement is a useful practice proposal, but does not guarantee truthfulness.

Appropriate reliance depends on context and capability [R4](../research/REFERENCES.md). Decide what needs checking and what evidence would be sufficient for the consequence involved. A second model's agreement is not independent source verification.

## Activity: challenge a plausible claim

Fictional claim: “Longer AI conversations always improve learning.”

1. **5 min:** Record your initial view and confidence.
2. **10 min:** Separate the empirical claim from a preference for long conversations. Identify an observable outcome.
3. **10 min:** Ask for the strongest objection, an alternative explanation, and what evidence would discriminate. Offline, exchange these roles with a partner.
4. **10 min:** Open a source in the [reference file](../research/REFERENCES.md). Record the actual population, method, finding, and limit. If only an abstract is available, say so. Do not invent missing detail.
5. **5 min:** Write a qualified conclusion and identify remaining uncertainty.
6. **10 min:** Compare decisions and complete the exit check.

## Exit check

Submit the original claim, a checked source link, the passage or finding relevant to it, and a revised conclusion. Distinguish “unsupported,” “contradicted,” and “not yet checked.”

**Facilitator:** Participants need not manufacture disagreement with a supported claim. Reward a stated standard of evidence and a willingness to revise. If a citation fails to resolve, record it as unverified and seek a suitable source rather than accepting its appearance.

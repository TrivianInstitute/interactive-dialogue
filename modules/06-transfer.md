# 06 — Creativity and transfer

**50 minutes · Outcome:** explore possibilities and retain an independent contribution.

## Learn

Dialogue can help open a design space: analogies, different perspectives, unexpected constraints, and questions that reshape the task. Mark imaginative proposals as proposals. When they contain factual claims, check those separately.

Retaining authorship means recognizing what you supplied, what emerged through the exchange, and what you chose to carry forward. Include provenance when sharing work. For learning, also check whether you can explain and adapt the result outside the conversation.

## Activity: create, select, transfer

Task: design a welcoming sign and a five-minute introduction for a community learning event.

1. **5 min:** Independently draft one concept and define two criteria, such as clarity and belonging.
2. **10 min:** Invite three contrasting concepts, including one that challenges your first framing. Offline, use peers or invent the alternatives yourself.
3. **10 min:** Combine or reject ideas. Identify your own contribution and explain the choices. Check any factual claim before publication.
4. **10 min:** Close the exchange. Adapt the concept for a different setting: a library with no screens or a multilingual outdoor gathering.
5. **5 min:** Write a provenance note and one unresolved question.
6. **10 min:** Present both designs and complete the exit check.

## Exit check

Submit an original idea, alternatives, selection reasons, an independently adapted design, and provenance note. Distinctive voice matters; copying an elegant option does not demonstrate transfer.

**Facilitator:** Do not grade aesthetic conformity. Offer drawing, speech, dictation, and plain text. Transfer checks should change the context while preserving a comparable level of difficulty. Discuss whose perspective is missing from the design and how to invite actual feedback.

# Worked dialogues

Fictional teaching examples. They are not transcripts, measured outcomes, or model tests.

## 1. From answer to inquiry

**Person:** “Give me a plan for a neighborhood learning event.”

**AI:** “Run a one-hour webinar with a guest lecturer.”

**Person:** “Many neighbors lack steady internet. I was picturing a library table. What does your plan assume, and how might a low-tech version work?”

**AI:** “I assumed online access and a speaker. A library table could have small-group discussion cards.”

**Person:** “I can write the first card. I want each participant to contribute an example; how should the facilitator invite someone who prefers to observe?”

**Notice:** the person brought a constraint and a contribution. The next check is whether the plan works for the actual participants, preferably with their feedback.

## 2. Pleasant agreement is insufficient

**Person:** “Longer AI conversations always mean deeper learning.”

**AI:** “Absolutely. Every additional turn deepens understanding.”

**Person:** “That is too strong. What evidence distinguishes time spent talking from what a person can later explain alone?”

**AI:** “We would need an independent post-task. I cannot infer it from turn count.”

**Person:** “I will check an original study and qualify the claim. For this exercise, I will explain the idea without your response visible.”

**Notice:** a challenge prompt elicited a revision in this fictional exchange. In a real exchange, the person must still inspect the source and the independent work.

## 3. Agency can include accepting advice

**Person:** “I have two event formats. I value low cost and local access. Compare them, but do not contact anyone.”

**AI:** “The library table better meets both priorities. A webinar may reach distant participants.”

**Person:** “I agree with the library table for this event. I will ask participants about access needs before fixing the schedule. Help draft a short invitation for my review.”

**Notice:** agreement can be a reasoned choice. Drafting an invitation is distinct from sending it; any actual send requires authorization and a suitable system control.

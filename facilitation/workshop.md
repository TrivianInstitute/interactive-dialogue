# 90-minute workshop

**Audience:** older secondary learners and adults. Adapt to local context. No live AI access required; [fictional dialogues](../examples/worked-dialogues.md) and paper alternatives suffice.

## Prepare

Print [first dialogue](../activities/first-dialogue.md), [question cards](../templates/question-cards.md), [notebook](../templates/dialogue-notebook.md), and the [rubric](../assessment/rubric.md). Choose a checked, low-stakes task within facilitator competence. Offer accessible formats and quiet or paired participation.

## Run

| Time | Activity | Facilitator cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10 | Welcome, goals, consent to participation modes | Participants may pass; use fictional information |
| 10–25 | First dialogue | Ask what the person contributed |
| 25–40 | Question cards and ambiguous brief | Invite interpretations of “accessible” |
| 40–55 | Independent attempt, hint, fresh check | Record confidence before checking |
| 55–70 | Evidence and dissent | Distinguish source support from a persuasive answer |
| 70–82 | Boundary and repair | Distinguish drafting from external action |
| 82–90 | Exit reflection | Name learning, uncertainty, and a next attempt |

## Participation and care

Invite speech, writing, drawing, and observation. Do not require disclosure of private conversations, trauma, disability, or spiritual beliefs. Offer an ordinary pause for movement or rest without interpreting body states as truth detectors. Facilitate disagreement about claims without compelling anyone to argue against their identity or dignity.

## Collect only what is needed

Participants may submit a fictional or redacted notebook. Ask before retaining or publishing artifacts. If collecting evaluation data later, obtain suitable review and consent; see the [evaluation plan](../research/EVALUATION_PLAN.md). End with a concrete transfer task rather than a satisfaction poll alone.
